How to find my 3-paycheck months

If you're paid every two weeks, you've felt it: most months bring two paychecks, but every so often a third one shows up and the month suddenly feels roomy. Figuring out how to find my 3 paycheck months comes down to one simple fact about the biweekly calendar — plus one sneaky detail that trips up almost everyone who tries to count it by hand. This guide walks through both, so you can mark your extra-paycheck months with confidence instead of being surprised by them.

What a 3-paycheck month actually is

A biweekly paycheck lands every 14 days. There are 52 weeks in a year, so 52 ÷ 2 = 26 paychecks a year. If every month got exactly two, you'd only have 24. Those two leftover checks have to land somewhere — so twice a year, one month catches a third payday. Those are your 3-paycheck months: the two months where the every-two-weeks rhythm squeezes in an extra deposit.

Two quick clarifications, because people mix these up constantly:

  • Semi-monthly is not biweekly. If you're paid on set dates — say the 15th and the last day of the month — you get exactly 24 checks a year and never a 3-paycheck month. Three-paycheck months are a biweekly (and weekly) thing only.
  • Weekly works the same way, just more often. Paid every Friday? That's 52 checks a year, and four months end up with five paydays instead of the usual four.

The manual method: count your paydays across a calendar

You don't need any software to do this — a calendar and a few minutes will do. Here's the honest, do-it-yourself way to count paydays biweekly and spot the extra months:

  1. Find your next payday. Look at your last pay stub or bank deposit and note the exact date and weekday — for most people that's a Friday.
  2. Count forward every 14 days. From that payday, jump ahead exactly two weeks at a time and write each date on the calendar: same weekday, two rows down, all the way through December.
  3. Mark the months that hold three dates. Go month by month. Most will contain two paydays. Circle the two months that contain three — those are your 3-paycheck months.

A worked example makes it click. Say your next payday is Friday, January 9, 2026. Counting forward every 14 days through the year gives you this:

That's the whole manual method — and it works. But before you write those months in ink, there are two things it won't tell you on its own.

Your 3-paycheck months are personal — not a universal list

Here's where a lot of "which months have 3 paychecks" articles lead people astray: they publish one set of months as if it applies to everybody. It doesn't. Your 3-paycheck months depend on the exact date and weekday of your payday, and that differs from person to person — even between coworkers.

Take two people who are both paid "every other Friday." If their pay weeks are offset by a single week, their extra-paycheck months are completely different. In the example above, a first payday of January 9 produced May and October. Shift the whole schedule back by one payday — a first check on Friday, January 2, 2026 instead — and the third checks land in January and July. Same "biweekly Friday" label, same employer even, totally different answer.

So the two common Friday scenarios you'll see quoted online — one dropping its extra checks around January and July, the other around May and October — are just two examples out of many possible schedules. They're a starting point, not your answer. The pitfall the manual method misses: weekend and holiday shifts

Even if you count perfectly, a hand-drawn calendar can still hand you the wrong month. Here's why: your scheduled payday isn't always your paid date. When a payday falls on a weekend or a federal holiday, most employers deposit the money on the previous business day. That small shift can quietly move a paycheck from one month into another — and change which month gets the third check.

The classic case happens at the turn of the year. Imagine a biweekly Friday schedule with paydays on December 4 and December 18, 2026, and the next one due Friday, January 1, 2027. But January 1 is New Year's Day, a federal holiday — so that check is deposited on Thursday, December 31, 2026 instead. Now December has three actual deposits (the 4th, 18th, and 31st), which makes December — not January — the real 3-paycheck month. A plain 14-day count would have filed that check under January and missed the extra month entirely.

Weekend shifts do the same thing on a smaller scale all year long. A payday scheduled for the 1st of a month that happens to fall on a Saturday gets paid on the Friday before — the last day of the previous month. If you budget by when the money actually hits your account (and you should), those shifts are exactly what decides your true 3-paycheck months.
